Level 3 — inside each part

What each part is made of

The same four parts, opened up one layer. Each component below is a distinct job with a clear boundary — nothing here does two things at once.

Capture & recognition

  • Sound pickupCaptures the voice and passes it on as a clean signal. Whatever detail is lost at this point cannot be recovered by anything downstream, which makes it the quiet ceiling on the whole system's accuracy.
  • Live text conversionTurns the incoming speech into words on screen continuously, rather than waiting for the sentence to end. This is the part that makes dictation feel immediate instead of delayed.
  • Cleanup passTakes a second look at the finished text before the radiologist ever sees it. It ignores style and wording entirely and checks only the handful of error types that change clinical meaning.

Vocabulary & personalization

  • Saved word listHolds the terms this radiologist actually uses — drug names, devices, eponyms, referring clinicians, local place names. Without it, the system falls back to whatever common word sounds closest, which is where most "it never gets my accent" complaints really come from.
  • Personal voice profileThe accumulated result of everything this person has corrected so far, applied to how their speech is read. It is small, individual, and completely separate from the model everyone else shares.

Correction & review

  • Final check before signingA deliberate look for the specific errors that read as perfectly plausible on the page. These are the ones a normal proofread misses, because the sentence still makes sense when it is wrong.
  • Fix captureRecords every correction in full at the moment it is made. This step does double duty — it is a safety action for the patient and, at the same time, the only source of learning data the system will ever receive.

Training & adaptation

  • Overnight updateCollects the day's corrections and uses them to adjust this one person's profile. It runs unattended, once a night, rather than continuously — so a single odd moment mid-shift can never destabilise recognition while someone is working.
  • Quality checkDecides whether the new update is allowed near tomorrow's dictation. Nothing goes live on the assumption that more learning is automatically better; it has to be demonstrated first.
Level 4 — the detail that matters

The parts worth knowing precisely

Six components where the specifics decide whether the whole thing works. Each is broken into the same shape: what it does, and the few decisions inside it that carry real weight.

Sound pickup

This sets the ceiling for everything else. It is a physical problem, not a software one.

  • A microphone that rejects noise from the sidesA reading room is full of other conversations and equipment. A directional microphone hears the person speaking rather than the room around them, which removes a whole category of error before it ever reaches the software.
  • The same position every sessionSame distance, same angle, every time. The system learns how this person sounds from one fixed vantage point, so a microphone that drifts across the day quietly undoes that learning.
  • A wired connectionA stable, uncompressed signal gives the system the full detail of the voice. Wireless links compress audio to save bandwidth, and the detail they discard is exactly what distinguishes similar-sounding words.

Live text conversion

Where speed and accuracy meet. Done properly, one does not cost the other.

  • Text streams out continuouslyWords appear the moment they are said, so the radiologist can follow along as they dictate rather than waiting and then reviewing a wall of text.
  • Early guesses are allowed to changeA word can be revised a moment later once the rest of the phrase makes the meaning obvious. This is what lets it be both fast and accurate — it commits early but is not stuck with it.
  • A strong shared model does the heavy liftingIt already understands general speech and radiology language well before anything personal is applied. Personalization is a refinement on top of something good, not a rescue of something weak.
  • This person's profile sits on topThe shared model handles language; the personal profile handles this specific voice. Keeping them separate is what allows one to improve nightly without disturbing the other.

Cleanup pass

Deliberately narrow. It only looks at error types with real clinical consequences.

  • Numbers and unitsA measurement in the wrong unit is never a harmless typo — it changes the finding. Comparisons against a prior study are the highest-risk case of all.
  • Left and rightThese read as entirely plausible when wrong, which is exactly what makes them dangerous. The sentence stays grammatical and sensible, so nothing about it prompts a second look.
  • Negative wordsDropping a single "no" turns a normal finding into an abnormal one. They are short, unstressed, and easy to lose in fast speech, which is a poor combination for the most meaning-carrying word in the sentence.
  • Nothing else is touchedWording and style are left exactly as dictated. Restricting the pass this tightly is what guarantees it can only remove errors, never introduce new ones of its own.

Saved word list

Most errors people blame on their accent are simply words the system was never told about.

  • Words are favoured at the moment of recognitionNothing is retrained to add one. A word added now works on the very next sentence, which is what makes it realistic to maintain the list during real work.
  • Each radiologist has their own listOne person's referring clinicians and local place names are not another's. A shared list would grow noisy and start causing errors of its own.
  • A shared radiology list sits underneathThe common vocabulary of the specialty is there from day one for everyone, so no individual has to teach the system the basics of their own field.

Fix capture

All recorded automatically. Deleting the words and saying them again records none of it — which is exactly why the correction tool matters.

  • The short piece of audioSo the system can listen again to what was actually said rather than guess from text alone. Without the audio, a correction shows what was wrong but not why.
  • What the system first wroteThe wrong text, kept so the mistake itself can be studied. Knowing what it reached for reveals which words it confuses with which.
  • What the radiologist changed it toThe correct text — the actual thing to be learned. This is the target the overnight update trains toward.
  • The words just before and afterSo the fix is learned in its real context rather than as an isolated word swap. The same word can be right in one sentence and wrong in another, and only the surrounding words tell them apart.

Overnight update

Small, targeted, and never affecting what anyone else uses.

  • Collect today's fixes, plus the last few daysA single day rarely holds enough corrections to reveal a real pattern. Widening the window slightly means the update learns habits rather than one afternoon's oddities.
  • Set aside the ones that teach nothingDuplicates of the same fix and anything with no matching audio behind it are dropped. Repeating one correction many times would skew the update toward that single word.
  • Adjust only this person's profileThe shared model underneath is left completely untouched. This is what makes nightly updates safe to run — the blast radius of a bad night is one individual, not the whole hospital.
  • Hold back a portion of the fixesSome corrections are deliberately kept out of the update, so there is something honest left to test it against afterwards. Testing on material it already learned from would prove nothing.

Quality check

The safeguard that lets the whole loop run overnight with nobody watching it.

  • Test it on the fixes it was never shownThe portion held back during the update. This is a fair test rather than a rehearsed one, and it answers the direct question: did it actually learn today's mistakes?
  • Test it on ordinary everyday speech tooCommon words and phrases unrelated to today's corrections. This catches the failure where a system gets better at a handful of new words by getting quietly worse at everything else.
  • Compare both results against yesterday's versionThe update has to improve, or at minimum not regress, on both tests. One improving while the other slips is not a good trade.
  • Both fine, it goes live tomorrowThe radiologist notices nothing at all except that it works slightly better than it did yesterday.
  • Either one worse, yesterday's version staysThe update is discarded and the previous profile remains in place. One strange day of dictation can never quietly make tomorrow worse.
The whole thing

The loop, in one page

Clean audio in. A directional microphone in a consistent position gives the system the best possible signal — nothing downstream can recover what is lost here. Live text out. Words appear as they are spoken, shaped by this radiologist's saved word list and personal voice profile, then checked by a narrow cleanup pass for numbers, laterality and negative words. The radiologist corrects. Every fix is captured in full — the audio, the wrong text, the right text, the surrounding words. Overnight, it learns. The day's fixes adjust that one person's profile, a held-back portion tests whether it actually improved, and the update only goes live if it did. Tomorrow starts slightly better than today, every day, without anyone managing it.
