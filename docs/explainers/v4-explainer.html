A. Live audio: cut into chunks, each decoded exactly once

The mic stream at 16 kHz. When the VAD hears a pause, the chunk closes, gets decoded once and is frozen ("committed"). The still-open tail is re-decoded every 0.5 s just to show a live preview, and that preview is never committed.
committed chunk (decoded once, frozen) open tail (preview only) VAD cut: 0.6 s silence, chunk ≥ 2 s; aims for 5 s, forced at 20 s stop → low-confidence chunks re-decoded (polish < 0.75)

C. What the model actually is

Base
NVIDIA Parakeet-TDT 0.6B v3. The app's "standard" is v2 (picked because v3 is worse at English), so v4 and standard are different base models
Encoder
FastConformer, 24 layers, 2.44 GB in both v2 and v4: same compute
Decoder + joint
TDT: predicts the next word piece and how many frames to skip. v4's is 72 MB with 8,192 word pieces; v2's is 36 MB with 1,024
Runs as
ONNX on CPU via onnx-asr, fp32, greedy
Speed
Not reliably measured. One timing pass per model, PC short of RAM: 6.1 to 8.0 s per audio minute for both on 3 sets, 22 to 92 on others (paging). A clean re-test ran out of memory (2.7 of 14.8 GB free). Same encoder, so a big difference is unlikely, but not proven
Lives in
data/models/fine_tuned/parakeet-rad-v4/, offline, nothing leaves the PC

E. After you press stop

Polish runs in the background and is thrown away if you edit the report meanwhile. On release, the gate asks one question about unfilled [fields], side, sex and a missing impression, then runs the critical-findings check (NegEx). It never blocks the report, it only changes what goes in the audit log.

Scores on held-out audio (lower is better)

Same clips for every model, scored through the app's own engine code. eka = real Indian-accented clinicians (140 clips), noise/quiet = the same clips with pink noise or turned down. libri = everyday read English. parrot = radiology reports in TTS voices.

Did the test leak into training?

A test score only means something if the model never heard those sentences. Every training line is saved in train_texts.txt and checked against every test set.

How the quality is protected along the whole path

Honest caveats

Two different "stocks". Local scores compare against Parakeet v2 (the app's standard). The Kaggle check in step 7 compares against v3, v4's own base. They're different models, so don't mix the two tables.
Hotwords untested. Where sherpa-onnx is installed (the full parakeet extra), "standard" becomes v2 + radiology hotwords. v4 has never been scored against that. On this PC it isn't installed, so the comparison here is fair for this PC only.
The ranges are a bit too narrow. Resampling is by clip, but eka's 140 clips come from fewer speakers, and clips from one speaker aren't independent. About 12 comparisons were made, so one "beyond luck" result at 95% could be chance. eka_quiet medical words (upper end −0.53) is the most fragile. eka WER is the most solid.
Test sets reused across versions. eka has been used to judge v1 → v4, so recipe choices have slowly fitted it. Your own voice is the first truly fresh test.
Term error favours the tuned model. Medical words are counted from the app's lexicon, and the synthetic training lines were built from that same lexicon.
parrot is flattering. Its test voices are the same Kokoro TTS voices v4 trained on (different sentences). Only eka and libri count toward the decision.
Everyday English is +0.49 worse than stock (range −0.11 to +1.16), over the agreed +0.3 limit, though it ties v3. Fine-tuning on medical speech costs a bit of general English.
Noise didn't improve. The training noise (babble, hiss) differs from the test noise (pink) on purpose. Real reading-room noise is untested.
